The daily brief for
early‑stage founders.

Every morning it tells you what changed in your company yesterday, and what to do about it today. One email. Thirty seconds.

Micah Okoko · micahokoko@gmail.com

The problem

You check six tools every morning and still don’t know what changed.

GitHub knows what shipped. Stripe knows who paid. Your database knows who signed up. Each tool answers its own question. None of them answer yours: what happened, and what should I do today?

So you stop checking. A bug sits for two weeks. A channel that was working dies and you find out at the end of the month.

The solution

One email every morning. Here is what changed.

Signups +18% 47
Pull requests merged — 6
Activation −7% 29%

Activation fell the day the onboarding form shipped. Roll it back, or split the step.

No dashboard. Nothing to log into. You read it and you are done.

How it works

Built. Running nightly.

collectGitHub — merged PRs, commits, deploys, open PRs
collectSupabase — new signups
collectPlausible — visitors, where they came from
collectStripe — revenue, new customers
comparecode works out every number
writeAI turns it into sentences
checkevery number is matched back to the data
sendone page, one email

Setup takes fifteen minutes. We only count rows in your database. We never read what is inside them.

The insight

The AI never makes up a number.

Every number is worked out by code, then checked against the raw data before the email goes out. The AI only writes the sentences. If the data does not explain why something happened, the brief says so.

An AI that gets one number wrong gets deleted. Trust is the whole product.

Why we win the entry point

Every other tool needs traffic and revenue first. We don’t.

Mixpanel and Amplitude only become useful once you have real volume. An early founder has code, deploys, a few signups and a landing page. That is exactly what we read.

So we are useful on day one. By the time a founder is big enough to buy Mixpanel, we are already installed. The big tools cannot come down here without breaking their own product.

They show you data. We tell you what to do.

Why it gets harder to copy

Every brief adds to a record nobody else can rebuild.

  1. 01Each brief pairs a change with what happened next. Over time that becomes a history of what actually works.
  2. 02Across thousands of companies it becomes a benchmark. Your activation is 32%. Similar startups run 41%. No founder can work that out alone.
  3. 03None of it exports. Leaving means losing your own history.

The first version is easy to copy. Year three is not. The data is the asset, not the email.
